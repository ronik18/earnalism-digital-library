## Art direction and composition

- Make the subject visually substantial in each independently composed format. Avoid the default of a small central icon, large empty background and heading. Keep negative space when it directs attention or serves captions; richness is intentional detail, not filling every pixel.
- Choose a coherent topic-specific palette with luminous highlights, clear value contrast and controlled accents. Festivals may invite jewel tones and warm practical lights; another subject may need a quieter palette. Colourfulness does not require oversaturation, constant glow or poor text contrast. Do not encode essential distinctions through colour alone.
- Use foreground, subject and background depth where it adds atmosphere or explains relationships. Texture, lighting, reflections and material detail should support the focal idea. Keep characters, objects and environments consistent between shots.
- Include people, gestures, expressions and recognisable stakes when they belong to the story. Use accurate respectful depictions; avoid inserting faces into mechanisms that diagrams explain better.
- Use original or appropriately licensed high-quality illustrations and footage when useful. Select image-generation tools for illustrative assets when available and authorized; a renderer's geometric primitives must not become the artistic ceiling. Keep rights/provenance and inspect generated anatomy, lettering, symbols and historical details. Existing spending boundaries apply.
