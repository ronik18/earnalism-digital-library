# From a browsing choice to a worthwhile lesson

Use this topic-agnostic planning and review contract for every future subject. The simulated frequent-viewer critique supplies hypotheses about relevance, clicking, understanding and returning; it supplies no real participants, global ranking, audience statistics or guaranteed outcome. Read [coverage](../COVERAGE.md) to trace each quality to an action and evidence. Use [audience and distribution](AUDIENCE_AND_DISTRIBUTION.md) for packaging files, manual YouTube checks and measured results rather than duplicating that workflow.

The planner and standards apply across topics. The actual current Remotion renderer implements lunar diagrams only. A different topic needs its own researched, tested explanatory model and appropriate scenes before rendering; replacing Moon labels is not general video generation. Record unsupported rendering capability as a hold, not a completed artifact. For existing editions, preserve the selected B voice and accepted soundtracks. Future topics use their own scripts with the selected original B direction and appropriate original scoring; never relabel or reuse Moon speech/music as another topic. New packaging or attention goals do not authorize retiming, resynthesis, celebrity imitation, gratuitous music changes or spending.

## Plan the viewer's reason to stay

Record the intended viewer's prior knowledge, question or misconception, the new understanding promised, and the consequence that makes it useful. Do not assume beginners and knowledgeable viewers want the same novelty. A familiar subject can have a distinctive comparison or revealing mechanism; an unfamiliar subject still needs orientation. State what the viewer should be able to explain or predict after watching, and what remains outside the episode. Creators fill ordinary choices autonomously from research and scope; human approval fields remain empty until a real review occurs.

For **every scene**, record the following in the implemented audience/viewer plan:

| Plan item | Concrete decision | Review evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Viewer question | What uncertainty does this scene resolve, given the previous scene? | Reviewer can name its purpose without relying on a generic heading. |
| Explanatory visual answer | Which object, comparison, transformation or evidence makes the answer visible? Bind it to source claims and the topic model. | Actual final frames/motion demonstrate the relation; a moving ornament, stock picture or text paragraph alone does not establish explanation. |
| Bridge | What newly visible fact or remaining question carries attention into the next scene? | Referents and state remain coherent across the cut; the next scene follows the reasoning instead of restarting it. |
| Novelty and load | What is new for this audience, what can be removed, and how much reading/inspection time is needed? | Inspect actual labels/captions at narrow and fullscreen sizes; listen to the unchanged approved speech. |
| Format and timing | What changes between independently composed landscape and Shorts, and which actual-word/event anchors govern the reveals? | Each format has a complete payoff and uncluttered hierarchy; no blind crop, unspoken caption or unsupported timing. |

Keep scene count, graphic technique and timing appropriate to the subject. A scene may answer a question through a controlled contrast, an accurate chart, a schematic mechanism or a source comparison. Do not force every episode into Moon phases, identical diagrams or an arbitrary visual style.

## Earn the opening and the payoff

Give the first visible frame a meaningful subject, readable hierarchy and a question worth resolving. Make title and thumbnail complement each other: one clear focal idea and minimal necessary copy, rather than two long questions. Keep depicted evidence truthful. Require actual cover assets and small readbacks; a brief or full-size scene still alone is insufficient. Do not use false urgency, invented findings or misleading imagery to manufacture curiosity.

Plan a specific early explanatory payoff and its time based on the subject, prior knowledge and accepted narration. Inspect the actual first frame, first consequential visual event and payoff in the final exports. There is no universal number of seconds that guarantees attention. Preserve the approved brand ending; do not delay learning with an added ident or accelerate B to meet a hook target.

Where useful, include one prediction, retrieval or application opportunity: give sufficient inspection/reading time, then show an accurate answer or consequence. It should deepen the explanation, not become an arbitrary quiz or additional text layer. Check it against actual audio, labels, captions and visual transition times. An in-video question does not replace the separate neutral comprehension study; keep study questions/answer keys away from participants before their one viewing.

Three unrelated planning illustrations follow. They are design examples, not researched or rendered episodes; verify all claims and build/test the relevant model before production.

| Topic and audience uncertainty | Possible visual answer and bridge | Useful evidence of understanding |
|---|---|---|
| Bread making: what changes inside dough, beyond its visible size? | A sourced cutaway connects a measured dough change to the proposed mechanism; bridge from the hidden process to a controlled comparison. Label any schematic honestly. | Viewer explains the mechanism rather than merely remembering that dough gets bigger; inspect the comparison for omitted conditions. |
| Simple electric circuits: why does changing one connection matter? | A tested circuit model links a specific wiring change to the observable result, keeping the same components visible; bridge to a prediction using a second configuration. | Viewer predicts the new configuration and explains it; no visual current metaphor is presented as a literal physical simulation. |
| Historical maps: what can two maps establish, and what can they not establish? | Rights-cleared dated maps align the relevant evidence while preserving provenance, legend and uncertainty; bridge from apparent difference to the source limitation. | Viewer distinguishes a depicted change from a stronger unsupported historical conclusion. |

## Give a reason to return without inventing a catalogue

Define a repeatable series promise around a type of learning value, not a claim that every video is extraordinary. End with an earned callback or application and an honest next step. Link to a verified available lesson; label a genuinely planned lesson as planned; otherwise record that none is available. A standard ending or generic “subscribe” request alone does not explain what a returning viewer will receive.

Let real unanswered questions, comprehension difficulties and relevant requests shape a candidate topic backlog. Record source, frequency/sample limits, audience fit, factual risk, render capability and production effort; a noisy comment or presumed trend is not automatic priority. Do not contact viewers or collect new personal data without authorization. Choose a sustainable cadence from measured production/review cost and available capacity. A cadence plan is not automatic scheduling, permission to publish unfinished work or authorization for paid compute.

## Test choices and outcomes honestly

For a real card-choice study, show the target title/thumbnail among plausible relevant distractors of comparable finish, with randomized order and comparable display size. Use rights-cleared study materials. Participants may choose any card or none. Do not name the desired answer, praise the target, disclose a supposed ranking or coach the choice. Record anonymous participant IDs, prior exposure/topic knowledge, displayed cards/order/size, first choice and their unprompted reason. Keep card-choice, full-watch/listening and one-view comprehension evidence separate. A hypothetical agent choice is not a participant record or click-through result.

Use actual format/traffic-source results at the configurable checkpoints in the distribution guide. Compare packaging choice, early retention, explanation confusions and return/next-step behavior with the plan and real participant comments. Preserve denominators, small-sample limits and failed hypotheses. Metrics locate questions; they do not independently identify causes or prove lasting learning. Keep missing evidence unknown and choose one justified next experiment rather than endless speculative voice/music polishing.

Automatic checks can enforce plan coverage, asset/timing bindings, readable-caption thresholds and the existence of actual render evidence. They cannot certify novelty, desire to click, musical sophistication, human naturalness, legal compliance, perfection, loyal subscribers or revenue. Strict factual/graphical and actual listening approvals remain mandatory. Reuse the existing player, rights and publication gates; no new dashboard, CMS, payment system, bulk production or automatic upload follows from this reference.
