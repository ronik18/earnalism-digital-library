# Series planning and a lean 90-day growth cycle

Use when asked to plan a series, launch a channel or connect episodes to audience/business results. For an ordinary episode, inherit the relevant audience promise and next step without regenerating a 90-day plan. [GO LIVE](GO_LIVE.md) remains authoritative; marketing experiments are advisory, not additional release gates.

## Decide the audience and product connection

Inspect the actual live product and authorized available catalogue before choosing the series. State a specific audience hypothesis, recurring learning promise and honest path from episode to a relevant product destination. Earnalism has Bengali/English reading and released audiobooks; verify current access terms and each title's release before advertising it. Broad science reach is not evidence of library purchase intent. Respect the user's chosen topic; explain a weak commercial connection rather than silently replacing it. Do not impose literature, English, an age group or a fixed duration on all future series.

## Prepare a small connected season

Choose a sustainable pilot, often six episodes. For each, create: selected truthful title, opening question, meaningful first-frame subject and early visible answer, observable learning outcome, topic-specific explanatory animation, concise thumbnail concept, independently staged portrait insight(s), research/rights cautions and a relevant next step with available/planned/absent status. A brief is not a finished script, rendered asset or clearance. A repeated visual grammar is useful; interchangeable substance is not. Mark interpretation, reconstruction and schematic views appropriately.

Each short should deliver a complete insight, not merely withhold the answer. Allow deeper landscape treatment only when the subject warrants it; never pad for watch hours. Keep the accepted voice identity and original provider. Do not trigger new auditions or paid generation from a planning task. Preserve existing media unless changes are authorized. Keep exact branding and the approved END ident; do not add campaign slogans to standalone brand introductions.

## Turn the season into dated work

Use a dated 90-day window and explicit timezone. Start with source/product qualification and first-episode preparation, then a sustainable release cadence, then cohort review and conditional follow-ups. Calendar rows should distinguish preparation, qualification, proposed publication and observation; dates are hypotheses, not proof of scheduling or authority to publish. Make production dependencies visible. If quality/capacity fails, move the date or reduce cadence. Do not automate posting, purchase reach, send outreach or create remote calendar events from a plan alone.

Use existing tools: a short plan, episode briefs and CSV/calendar only when helpful. No new dashboard/CMS, bulk framework or tracking stack. In a calendar export, use tentative private events and avoid invites or automatic actions. Current local example: project `output/strategy/90-day-2026-10-30/`; its story choices and dates are a historical pilot, not defaults for other topics.

## Learn from observed results

Use native analytics at comparable 7/28-day ages as a starting convention. Record source, date/window, format, denominator and unknowns. Separate CTR by traffic source; inspect early/scene retention, returning viewers, subscriber gains, available short/reel engagement, relevant website visits/purchases and actual production cost. Use existing measurement; a tagged URL is not proof of conversion tracking. Do not mix impressions, reach, views, engaged/qualified views and unique people. Avoid universal viral benchmarks. Compare small pilot cohorts directionally; do not claim statistical significance or lifetime value without adequate evidence. Trial Reels are public, not private QA.

Use actual misunderstanding/abandonment to choose the next correction or follow-up. Optional packaging variants, neutral viewer studies and collaborations can inform growth but cannot create redundant approval loops. Do not infer permission to message collaborators. Distinguish observed results from illustrative funnel assumptions, forecasts and ambitions. Check changing platform policy/tool/monetization claims against official sources at use; do not bake thresholds into enduring skill defaults. Optimize attributable contribution and sustainable output, not follower counts alone; no guaranteed retention or revenue.

## When a series trailer is requested

Make a short standalone demonstration of the learning experience, usually 20–35 seconds: a meaningful first-frame question, a visible explanatory payoff, one concrete recurring promise and a clear route to an actually available first lesson. Use a channel/profile feature position after authorized publication; do not prepend it to episodes or make it a release requirement. Never imply that planned lessons are already available.

Reuse verified original scenes and accepted voice excerpts when they form complete, coherent thoughts. Preserve audio-bound timing/provenance and check the new edit's joins and captions; reuse does not imply acceptance of the new montage. New narration still follows the existing provider/spending rules. Characters, motion and graphical spectacle must explain or motivate attention, rather than compete with the mechanism. Keep approved branding exact and the END ident last, without added campaign text inside the ident. Do not promise universal appeal, lifelong retention or revenue. Apply the same one final-candidate decision, with no new approval loop or automatic posting.

### Review excerpt-based trailers without chasing the mix

Check each voice splice in context, including the preceding and following thought, against the dry narration and encoded scored delivery. Short-term loudness matching can correct a measured level jump; it does not repair timbre, phrasing or emotional continuity. Preserve accepted voice samples, timing and pitch. With an authorized static-gain correction, retain original excerpts and verify equal sample counts and only expected PCM rounding; do not add generic crossfades that can erase consonants. The bundled [splice diagnostic](../software/series_trailer/audio_review.py) helper reports sample discontinuities and preservation, never perceptual acceptance. A quiet splice is not proof that a join sounds natural.

Inspect the actual score stem and authored rests at the new edit's passage boundaries, voice-free invitation and ident handoff. Do not copy an episode mix approval into a trailer or normalize every window to the same level. Retain purposeful silence; raise music only for a specific demonstrated audibility defect and audition the result. If listening is unavailable, state that limit and leave listening pending rather than claiming an audible review or making speculative gain changes. Give reviewers scored/dry playback and exact join times within the one consolidated decision.

Keep the subject visible from the first frame, demonstrate the opening promise promptly, synchronize viewpoint changes with their spoken instruction, and hold a revealed view when a repeated reset adds no explanation. Clamp delayed animation progress at both ends; test pre-start and post-end values. Give the series invitation adequate reading time and distinguish an intentionally unvoiced visual promise from spoken content. These are editorial checks, not extra approval forms or mandatory narration regeneration.

For an unvoiced closing, budget the text and reveal sequence together. Prefer one concise promise or next step, remove repeated subtitles, and expose the invitation early enough for comfortable reading after the final reveal. Shorten idle holds before changing speech speed. A five-second closing is a useful candidate for a simple promise, not a universal limit; verify both layouts and reading time. Preserve the narrated prefix and authored rests, fade only the speech-free score tail into the unchanged END ident, and verify PCM preservation rather than claiming a newly encoded AAC stream is byte-identical.

### Keep montage typography and copied audio verifiable

Do not inherit a full episode’s repeated supporting lines into a short montage when its heading, picture and captions already communicate the instruction. Provide scene-scoped typography overrides so a trailer can enlarge essential object labels without altering the source episode. Review final encoded frames at narrow landscape and actual portrait player sizes, with all delivered captions visible; font size at export resolution alone is insufficient. Preserve schematic context and speech captions while removing redundant prose.

For a visual-only repair, preserve the existing soundtrack with stream-copy muxing and compare the **complete extracted audio stream**, not only its narrated prefix. A video-frame output cap can truncate the audio tail even with copy enabled. Check final video/audio durations and the ending after finishing; reject and isolate a truncated attempt. Explicitly sized caption layers need measured text bounds, unchanged actual-audio cues and no overlap at transitions. Use available installed rendering dependencies; do not claim a subtitle filter ran when the host lacks it. These are focused acceptance checks, not another approval stage.

## Prepare a concrete launch package

When launch preparation is requested, resolve the current candidate pointer and bind copy, covers and settings to its verified manifest. Keep new packaging in a separate writable folder; never rewrite the qualified media or its approval records just to add a thumbnail. Produce one readable landscape thumbnail, a separately laid-out portrait cover with essential content safe in likely grid crops, exact YouTube/Shorts/Instagram copy, a concise settings sheet and a tentative timezone-explicit CSV calendar. Show one review page containing the exact videos and package. Mark local drafts, proposed schedules, actual uploads and actual publication separately.

Select audience classification per episode, including explicit owner confirmation where required; a prior brand-ident classification does not transfer to a science episode. Apply current AI-disclosure rules to the actual media. Never invent a related-video ID, product availability or account monetization eligibility. Exclude unpublished next-lesson links. At authorized delivery, verify platform processing, captions, copyright, the real public Reel/grid cover and copy. The package uses the existing one final-candidate decision, not additional marketing approvals. Reuse valid unchanged media evidence, state listening/factual holds truthfully, and treat calendar times as testable audience hypotheses rather than reach guarantees.

## Release remains one decision

Primary factual, graphical/caption, rights, real listening/A/V and technical/delivery evidence → one exact-candidate consolidated decision → authorized delivery and readback. Reuse unchanged evidence. Known defects still block; optional growth work does not. Keep private until the authorized release. A plan, calendar import or high self-score is never publication approval.


### Match spoken references to visible evidence

When narration says “the three”, “this”, “watch the light” or another visual instruction, identify the exact visible referents and the on-screen action at that audio cue. A correct underlying model is insufficient when the relationship is barely visible. Distinguish an apparent/projected alignment from a physical three-dimensional alignment; a projected guide must disappear or transform before the viewpoint reveals a miss. A lit-edge highlight is not a depiction of incoming sunlight: use an appropriately directed, labelled schematic cue when the instruction requires it. Verify cue timing and direction in both rendered layouts; never interpret “three bodies” as a required count of light rays. Preserve accepted speech when the defect is in the picture.


When a diagram path crosses a label's rectangle, foreground white glyphs alone do not clear it: strokes can remain visible through letter counters and spaces. Apply a bounded opaque label background after the diagram paths, check its clearance from moving bodies/context/captions, and inspect the encoded pixels. Keep this scoped to the affected composition; don't alter protected source episodes merely to repair a trailer.
